## 1. Executive summary

Ugandan secondary learners increasingly face a competency-based curriculum that expects them to interpret information, apply knowledge, solve problems and reason through unfamiliar real-world situations. Yet revision is still commonly organized around textbooks, static notes, past-paper PDFs, generic quizzes, coaching and broad AI chat tools. These resources can provide information, but they rarely maintain a reliable model of what an individual learner understands, why the learner is struggling or what the learner should do next.

Kasoma is an adaptive revision and mastery platform built for this gap. It combines a curriculum and competency graph, a reviewed assessment bank, continuous learner modelling, adaptive practice, scenario-based explanations, rubric-aligned grading and delivery through web and WhatsApp.

At maturity, Kasoma will function as a personalized learning-intelligence layer across revision, assessment and targeted remediation. Its defining capability will be:

> **Identify the specific competency a learner has not mastered, infer the likely misconception or prerequisite gap, and select the next intervention most likely to help—then verify whether it worked.**

Kasoma is not intended to replace teachers, schools or the curriculum. It will help learners revise with direction, help teachers see actionable patterns and help families support progress using evidence rather than study time alone.

## 2. The problem

### 2.1 Revision is abundant but poorly directed

A learner may have notes, textbooks, past papers, videos, teachers and AI answers yet still not know:

- what they genuinely understand;
- which weakness matters most;
- whether the weakness is conceptual, procedural or interpretive;
- what to revise today;
- which question is appropriately difficult;
- whether an explanation corrected the misconception; or
- whether knowledge will still be available later.

Most tools leave the learner to plan revision despite the learner having the least evidence and expertise to make that decision.

### 2.2 Marks hide the reason for failure

A score such as 58% does not reveal whether the learner struggles with diagram interpretation, translating scenarios into equations, prerequisite knowledge, calculation accuracy or written justification. Topic-level averages can hide these differences as well.

Kasoma needs to model performance across multiple dimensions:

> Topic × Concept × Competency × Question form × Difficulty × Context × Time

### 2.3 Static past-paper practice is insufficient

Past papers are useful, but chronological paper practice often repeats what a learner already knows and offers limited diagnosis. New assessment scenarios also require transfer, not memorization of familiar solutions. Past papers therefore need to become structured evidence within a broader mastery system rather than remain a folder of PDFs.

### 2.4 Generic AI has no dependable learner memory or assessment authority

A general chatbot may explain a topic, but it does not automatically possess an approved curriculum map, reliable source permissions, validated rubrics, calibrated scoring or a longitudinal learner model. It may also provide plausible but incorrect explanations or complete homework without creating understanding.

### 2.5 Teachers lack timely learning intelligence

Teachers often receive marks after an assessment but not a real-time map of class misconceptions and prerequisite gaps. With large classes, it is difficult to identify which learners need which intervention and whether the intervention worked.

### 2.6 Access conditions shape the product

Learners use different devices, data plans and channels. A solution that only works through a rich desktop interface will exclude part of the intended market. Kasoma must therefore be mobile-first, bandwidth-conscious and capable of maintaining continuity between web and WhatsApp.

## 3. Product thesis

Learning improves when the system repeatedly performs six tasks well:

1. **Diagnose** the learner's present knowledge state;
2. **Prioritize** the highest-value gap;
3. **Select** an appropriate intervention;
4. **Explain and practise** using relevant scenarios;
5. **Reassess** using an equivalent but unfamiliar item; and
6. **Update** the learner model using the new evidence.

Kasoma's thesis is that a curriculum-native, evidence-driven loop can produce more useful revision than undirected browsing, generic question banks or open-ended AI chat alone.

### North-star promise

> **Every learner receives a revision plan that continuously changes according to what they actually understand.**

### North-star product question

> **What should this learner do next, and what evidence shows that it helped?**

## 4. Intended users and jobs to be done

### Learners

- Find out what I do and do not understand.
- Know what to revise next without planning everything myself.
- Practise at the right difficulty.
- Understand why an answer is wrong.
- Prepare for competency-based questions and exam conditions.
- Continue revision on the device and channel available to me.

### Teachers

- See which concepts and competencies are weak across a class.
- Distinguish common misconceptions from isolated errors.
- Assign targeted practice.
- form intervention groups;
- measure whether remediation worked.

### Parents and guardians

- Understand progress and support needs without needing subject expertise.
- See evidence of improvement rather than screen time alone.
- receive proportionate alerts when a learner is falling behind.

### Schools and education leaders

- Monitor competency development across classes and subjects.
- Support teachers with actionable assessment evidence.
- identify persistent curriculum or teaching gaps;
- evaluate learning interventions over time.

## 5. The mature Kasoma experience

### 5.1 Onboarding and learner baseline

The learner selects level, subjects, goals and examination horizon. Kasoma begins with a short adaptive diagnostic rather than a long general examination. The diagnostic samples key concepts and competencies, then expands only where more evidence is needed.

The learner receives a profile such as:

- Waves — conceptual understanding: strong;
- Waves — diagram interpretation: developing;
- Electricity — application: weak;
- Algebra — translating scenarios into equations: weak;
- evidence confidence: moderate.

### 5.2 Daily mastery plan

Kasoma proposes a manageable plan based on importance, weakness, prerequisites, forgetting risk and available time. A recommendation is explained:

> Revise wave diagrams next because recent answers show that you know the formula but often extract wavelength incorrectly from diagrams.

Learners retain agency: they may follow the recommendation, choose a topic or enter Exam Mode.

### 5.3 Scenario-first learning

Abstract concepts are introduced through familiar, pedagogically appropriate situations—for example transport, rainfall, construction, markets, electricity use, agriculture or telecommunications. Localization is used to make transfer easier, not as decoration or stereotype.

A learning sequence may be:

> Scenario → Observation → Concept → Worked example → Guided practice → Independent question → Transfer question

### 5.4 Feedback that diagnoses

Kasoma goes beyond correct/incorrect. Feedback may identify:

- what the learner did correctly;
- which rubric element is missing;
- the misconception or prerequisite gap;
- an appropriate hint;
- a worked solution after effort; and
- a follow-up item that tests correction.

### 5.5 Mastery Mode

Mastery Mode is supportive and adaptive. It allows hints, scaffolding, explanations, retrieval practice, spaced repetition and repeated testing from different angles.

### 5.6 Exam Mode

Exam Mode is timed and realistic. It withholds hints, uses valid paper composition rules, applies approved marking guides and produces a post-exam competency breakdown with a targeted revision plan.

### 5.7 Visual and interactive learning

For concepts where movement or invisible processes matter, Kasoma provides simulations and manipulable diagrams. Each simulation has an explicit learning objective and ends with a transfer assessment. It is not treated as entertainment alone.

### 5.8 Guided capture: Snap & Learn

A learner may photograph a question, textbook excerpt, notes or handwritten working. Kasoma identifies the concept and supports the learner without becoming a copying engine. It may begin with a simpler related problem, request an attempt, diagnose the working and scaffold toward the original question.

### 5.9 Cross-channel continuity

Web provides the richest experience. WhatsApp provides quick diagnostics, reminders, short practice, feedback and progress summaries. Future native or offline experiences will use the same learner identity, content model and mastery evidence.

### 5.10 Teacher and school intelligence

Teachers see class-level competency heatmaps, misconceptions, learners requiring intervention and recommended groups. They can assign approved practice and compare before/after evidence. School leaders receive aggregated insights that protect individual learners and avoid simplistic ranking.

### 5.11 Parent support

Parent summaries emphasize progress, consistency and areas requiring support. They avoid public comparison, shame and false precision. Delivery may include WhatsApp summaries and actionable suggestions.

## 6. The learning intelligence architecture

Kasoma's mature product rests on six connected assets.

### 6.1 Curriculum and competency graph

The graph represents:

- levels, subjects, topics and concepts;
- learning outcomes and competencies;
- prerequisite and dependency relationships;
- question types and cognitive demand;
- assessment items, explanations and interventions.

This makes curriculum alignment queryable and allows Kasoma to distinguish a topic weakness from a competency or prerequisite weakness.

### 6.2 Structured assessment bank

Each assessment item should contain:

- curriculum and syllabus reference;
- subject, level, topic, concept and competency;
- prerequisite concepts;
- difficulty and expected time;
- response format;
- scenario/context metadata;
- answer key or scoring rubric;
- accepted variants and partial-credit rules;
- common misconceptions;
- hints, worked solution and remediation;
- equivalent or related items;
- source, permissions and provenance;
- author, reviewer, status and version.

### 6.3 Longitudinal learner model

The learner model estimates mastery and uncertainty for each relevant skill. It considers:

- correctness and partial score;
- item difficulty;
- independence and hints used;
- consistency across items and formats;
- recency and forgetting;
- prerequisite evidence;
- time and attempt behaviour where appropriate;
- performance on transfer questions.

Early versions are interpretable and rules-based. Later versions may evaluate Item Response Theory, Bayesian Knowledge Tracing, Deep Knowledge Tracing or other approaches, but only against a stable baseline and explicit learning metrics.

### 6.4 Intervention and recommendation engine

The engine selects the next activity by balancing:

- importance to curriculum/exam readiness;
- estimated weakness and uncertainty;
- prerequisite gaps;
- likelihood of forgetting;
- intervention effectiveness for similar errors;
- learner goals, time and channel;
- content diversity and fatigue.

Over time, Kasoma should learn not only what a learner does not know, but which type of intervention is most likely to help that learner master it.

### 6.5 Trustworthy grading and feedback

Kasoma uses the simplest reliable method for each response:

- exact or symbolic rules for objective/numerical work;
- approved rubrics for explanations;
- bounded model assistance for language variation;
- confidence thresholds and human-review routes;
- full audit evidence for important decisions.

### 6.6 Evidence and experimentation layer

Every recommendation, attempt, hint, explanation, score and reassessment becomes an event. This allows Kasoma to measure calibration, learning gain, delayed retention, transfer, fairness and intervention effectiveness. Data collection must remain proportionate, secure and explainable.

## 7. Role of AI, RAG and model training

### Retrieval-augmented generation

RAG should retrieve only approved curriculum, content and explanations. It can help answer questions with traceable context, but retrieval does not replace structured metadata, scoring rubrics or academic review.

### Generative AI

Appropriate uses include:

- explaining an approved concept in another way;
- adapting language and examples to the learner's level;
- generating candidate item variants for human review;
- extracting candidate metadata for reviewers;
- mapping free-text answers to bounded rubric evidence;
- summarizing progress for learners, teachers and parents.

### Deterministic systems

Rules and symbolic tools should handle operations where correctness can be established directly, including calculations, multiple-choice marking, units, accepted numeric ranges and progression constraints.

### Fine-tuning

Fine-tuning is not an initial requirement. It becomes justified when Kasoma has:

1. a specific weakness that prompting, retrieval and tools cannot solve adequately;
2. sufficient permissioned, high-quality examples;
3. a representative evaluation set; and
4. evidence that the trained model improves learning, quality or economics.

The valuable long-term dataset is not simply question-and-answer pairs. It is:

> Learner state → Item → Response → Error/misconception → Intervention → Reassessment → Learning outcome

## 8. Product principles

1. **Curriculum-native:** every learning activity maps to a curriculum outcome and competency.
2. **Mastery over activity:** learning evidence matters more than time spent or messages sent.
3. **Explainable adaptation:** learners and educators can understand recommendations.
4. **Human academic authority:** educators govern curriculum mapping, rubrics and publication.
5. **Use the simplest reliable intelligence:** rules before models when rules are sufficient.
6. **Low-bandwidth by design:** access constraints are product requirements.
7. **One learner, one evolving model:** evidence follows the learner across channels.
8. **Productive struggle:** support learning without doing all the thinking for the learner.
9. **Local relevance with integrity:** contexts aid understanding and never stereotype.
10. **Privacy and safety for minors:** collect less, protect strongly and design age-appropriately.
11. **Evidence before expansion:** new features must improve learning, access, trust or viability.
12. **Uncertainty is visible:** Kasoma does not present weak evidence as certain mastery.

## 9. Safety, privacy, fairness and academic integrity

### Safety and privacy

Kasoma should minimize personal data, apply age-appropriate consent, restrict internal access, encrypt data, define retention periods, provide deletion/export mechanisms and maintain a clear incident-response process.

### Fairness

Evaluation should test differences across gender, school type, location, language background, disability and device/connectivity conditions where ethically and statistically appropriate. A model that is accurate on average can still disadvantage a group.

### Academic integrity

Kasoma should distinguish revision support from answer provision. Hints should generally require an attempt. Snap & Learn should scaffold rather than immediately solve. Exam Mode must prevent instructional leakage during a timed assessment.

### Content and copyright

Every source must have documented provenance and permitted use. Possession of a paper or textbook does not automatically grant commercial reuse rights. Kasoma should maintain a rights register and distinguish source content, licensed content and original derivative items.

## 10. Outcomes and measurement

### Primary learner outcomes

- improved performance on unseen equivalent questions;
- delayed retention after meaningful intervals;
- improved transfer to unfamiliar scenarios;
- corrected misconceptions;
- better ability to plan and sustain revision;
- reduced gap between predicted and demonstrated mastery.

### Teacher and school outcomes

- earlier identification of learning gaps;
- more targeted interventions;
- reduced time required to assemble and mark routine practice;
- evidence of which interventions work;
- improved competency development at class level.

### Guardrail metrics

- grading errors and content flags;
- hallucination or unsupported-claim rate;
- harmful or inappropriate interactions;
- learner anxiety or unhealthy competition indicators;
- unequal outcomes across groups;
- support burden and cost per effective learning session.

Engagement is useful only when it supports learning. Kasoma should not optimize streaks, notifications or screen time at the expense of mastery or wellbeing.

## 11. Business and distribution model at maturity

Kasoma can serve multiple routes while preserving a common platform:

### Direct to learner/family

- free diagnostic or limited daily practice;
- affordable individual subscription;
- examination-preparation packages;
- sponsored access for underserved learners.

### Schools

- per-learner or school licence;
- teacher dashboards and assignments;
- onboarding, training and academic support;
- school-level analytics with appropriate privacy controls.

### Partners

- telecom/data partnerships;
- foundations and education programmes;
- publishers and licensed content providers;
- government or assessment partnerships where appropriate;
- institutional APIs and integration.

Business-model experiments must test willingness to pay, acquisition cost, support burden, model/infrastructure cost and measurable learning value. Institutional expansion should not cause the product to become a reporting system that neglects the learner experience.

## 12. Defensibility

Kasoma's durable advantage will not be exclusive access to a general-purpose model. It will come from the connected system:

- a trusted Uganda-specific curriculum and competency graph;
- a deeply reviewed and versioned assessment bank;
- longitudinal learner evidence across channels;
- mappings from misconceptions to effective interventions;
- academic operations and quality-control processes;
- trusted relationships with learners, teachers and schools;
- demonstrated learning outcomes.

The strongest long-term asset may be evidence showing which interventions improve mastery for which learner states. That is more difficult to reproduce than a chatbot interface.

## 13. Maturity horizon

### Horizon 1 — Prove adaptive revision

Kasoma proves the core loop for a narrow Senior 4 Mathematics and Physics scope through web and WhatsApp.

### Horizon 2 — Deepen mastery and exam readiness

Add spaced retrieval, flashcards, broader topic coverage, additional subjects, mini-mocks and full Exam Mode.

### Horizon 3 — Make difficult concepts observable

Introduce validated simulations, rich diagrams and guided capture where they improve conceptual transfer.

### Horizon 4 — Create school learning intelligence

Add teacher tools, class-level misconception maps, assignments, intervention groups and school reporting.

### Horizon 5 — Scale and learn across the system

Expand levels, subjects, offline access, parent support and institutional partnerships. Evaluate advanced learner models and recommendation methods using accumulated evidence.

The roadmap may change as market evidence grows, but each horizon should preserve the central mastery loop.

---

## 14. Strategic boundaries

Kasoma is:

- an adaptive revision and mastery system;
- a curriculum and assessment intelligence platform;
- a trusted bridge between learner evidence and the next best action.

Kasoma is not primarily:

- a generic chatbot;
- a PDF repository;
- a homework-answer engine;
- a replacement for teachers;
- a game optimized for attention;
- an automated authority that publishes unreviewed assessment content.

When a proposed feature conflicts with these boundaries, the team should either redesign it or explicitly revise this whitepaper through the version process.

## 15. Open questions for market validation

1. Which Senior 4 topics produce the highest combination of learner pain and assessment value?
2. Which explanation and feedback formats lead to the largest transfer gains?
3. How much diagnostic testing will learners tolerate before seeing value?
4. How should mastery and uncertainty be displayed so learners understand rather than fear them?
5. Which tasks work naturally on WhatsApp and which require web?
6. Who pays first: learners, parents, schools, sponsors or a combination?
7. What price and payment cadence fit household and school purchasing behaviour?
8. Which content licences or institutional relationships are required to scale safely?
9. What support do teachers need before they trust learner-level recommendations?
10. Which access barriers—device, data, language or digital skills—cause the greatest abandonment?
11. What learner and parent consent model is both safe and practical?
12. What measurable improvement is large enough for a school to adopt and renew?

These questions should become research tasks, not assumptions embedded permanently in the product.

## 16. Governance and versioning

- monthly during MVP and initial pilots;
- quarterly after product-market evidence stabilizes;

### Version rules

- **Minor (0.x / 1.x):** meaningful additions or changes to product capabilities, assumptions or market learning.

- date and owner;
- research or evidence considered;
- assumptions confirmed or rejected;
- strategic decisions changed;

## 17. North-star statement

Kasoma reaches maturity when it can reliably and safely tell a learner:

> **You are struggling with this specific competency, probably for this reason. This is the next activity most likely to help you, and here is the evidence that you improved.**

For teachers and schools, the same system should reveal where learning is breaking down and which interventions work—without reducing learners to marks, activity counts or opaque AI predictions.
